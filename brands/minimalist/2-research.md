### 2. Pain points (ranked)
1. **Blackheads, oiliness, bumpy texture.** The core job the product is bought for. Positive reviews mention "blackhead removal and smoother skin" ([search summary of brand & Nykaa reviews](https://www.nykaa.com/minimalist-2percent-salicylic-acid-face-serum-for-blackheads-whiteheads/reviews/15973485)).
2. **Fear of purging / "it made me break out".** The brand's own page says purging must be ridden out and suggests starting slowly. Estimates are 2–4 weeks across sources. Some reviewers "said their acne increased" ([brand](https://beminimalist.co/products/salicylic-acid-2), [dermfnd](https://dermfnd.org/p/minimalist-2-salicylic-acid-face-serum/)). This is a major objection to handle **honestly**.
3. **"Didn't work for me."** Some negative reviews report "no reduction in acne" ([Nykaa reviews](https://www.nykaa.com/minimalist-2percent-salicylic-acid-face-serum-for-blackheads-whiteheads/reviews/15973485)).
4. **Dryness / stickiness.** Users "found the serum slightly sticky in summer and noticed occasional dryness" ([Smytten](https://smytten.com/shop/product/serums-and-essence/2-salicylic-acid-serum-for-acne-blackheads-and-open-pores/MIN0013AB1)).
5. **Confusion about actives:** which one, how much, what to layer. Mintel says success "will hinge on simplifying the science" ([Mintel](https://store.mintel.com/report/india-skincare-ingredient-trends-market-report)).
6. **Fake products on marketplaces.** A general category concern (Which?: a majority of cosmetics from third-party marketplace sellers appeared fake) ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/at-least-two-thirds-of-cosmetics-we-bought-from-online-marketplaces-are-likely-counterfeits-a6DmA3P3BjhV)). This is UK data and not Minimalist-specific. ASSUMPTION that Indian shoppers share it; verify.

### 3. Desires
- Clearer-looking pores, fewer visible blackheads, smoother texture, less shine through the day.
- **Proof that it works.** Mintel: Millennials want "measurable efficacy"; Gen Z looks for "contextual efficacy" ([Mintel](https://store.mintel.com/report/india-skincare-ingredient-trends-market-report)).
- **Simplicity:** a short routine they understand.
- **Gentleness / barrier safety:** "gentle skincare focused on the skin barrier" and patch testing are rising ([OneIndia](https://www.oneindia.com/lifestyle/beauty-trends-2026-india-skin-hair-nail-fragrance-tools-011-8083769.html)).
- **Affordable enough to repurchase.**

| "Can I use it with niacinamide / retinol?" | Confusion (§2.5) | Routine guide content. Accuracy must be verified by the brand |

### 5. Review mining
- **Praise:** blackheads, smoother skin, less oily, lightweight.
- **Complaints:** purging, breakouts, no change, sticky in summer, dryness.
- **Language to mirror (verify verbatim first):** "blackheads", "bumpy texture", "oily by afternoon" (ASSUMPTION phrasing), "purging".
- **Rating context:** 4.05/5 is good, not perfect. Ads must quote it exactly, never round it up.

### 6. Trends
- **Shift from single heroes to systems/routines.** Elle India: "2026 is shaping up to be about systems. Not single hero ingredients" ([Elle India](https://elle.in/beauty/the-ingredients-that-will-actually-matter-this-year-10939848)). → Routine and kit messaging may resonate. Minimalist has an Anti-Acne Kit (₹1,019).
- **Efficacy-led, science simplified** ([Mintel](https://store.mintel.com/report/india-skincare-ingredient-trends-market-report)); dermo-cosmetics → "skinification" ([Ipsos](https://www.ipsos.com/en-in/beyond-beauty-emerging-skincare-trends-redefining-innovation)).

- **Gen Z** "open to trying what's trending, but confident enough to trust their own experience" ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/magazine/deep-dive/story/heres-how-gen-z-consumers-are-rewriting-indias-beauty-story-513230-2026-01-28)).
- **Market:** India skincare ≈ USD 10.3B in 2026, 7.5% CAGR ([IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/india-skincare-market)). Estimates vary widely by source.
- **Seasonal:** monsoon and summer humidity → oiliness peaks (ASSUMPTION — verify with Google Trends India for "salicylic acid serum"). Festive/wedding season (Oct–Dec) skin-prep content (ASSUMPTION).

### 8. Messaging gap
- **Likely saturated:** "2% salicylic acid for acne", "clinically proven", "dermatologist recommended", big % results. Every ingredient-led brand uses the same INCI-led language (ASSUMPTION — confirm in Ad Library).
- **Under-used opportunities:**
  1. **Radical honesty about purging and timelines.** Most brands avoid the "it might get worse first" conversation. Minimalist's own page already addresses it. This fits the transparency brand and also handles the #2 objection.
  2. **"Read the label with us."** Transparency as a demo: show the full INCI and %, explain each ingredient in simple words. This answers "simplify the science" (Mintel).
  3. **Routine, not a single bottle:** SA serum + B5 moisturizer + SPF 50, framed with the B2G3 offer. It rides the "systems" trend and fixes the price-per-ml disadvantage.
  4. **Gentle-but-effective:** patch testing + "start slow" guidance, matching the barrier-first trend.
- **Risks:** acne-reduction claims are regulated, and the consumer-study %s are unsubstantiated in ad form (see 1-brand §6–7). The winning gap (honesty) is also the safest one.
