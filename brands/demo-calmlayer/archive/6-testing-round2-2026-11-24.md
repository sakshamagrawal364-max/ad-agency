### 3. Confidence check
- **Purchases: 13 / 17 / 12 / 13.** All are below ~20–30 per ad, so **every CPA comparison is LOW CONFIDENCE.**
- Two-proportion z-tests (calculated):
  - B vs A, purchases ÷ impressions: p ≈ 0.42. **Not significant.** B's lower CPA could be noise.
  - B vs A, purchases ÷ clicks: p ≈ 0.89. Conversion after the click is essentially the same.
  - D vs A, purchases ÷ impressions: p ≈ 0.92. No difference.
  - **Hook rate B vs A: p < 0.001** and **D vs A: p < 0.001.** **HIGH CONFIDENCE** that both new hooks stop more scrolls than the control.
  - Hook rate C vs B: p ≈ 0.0002. C's opening is slightly stronger, but the gap is small (39.4% vs 37.7%).
  - **Cross-round:** R2C02's CVR (2.76%) vs Round 1 CL06 (2.05%): p ≈ 0.49. Not significant, and different weeks aren't a clean comparison anyway.
- Frequency is about 1.2 across all ads, so audience fatigue is unlikely to explain the differences yet.

### 4. Summary
- **Primary KPI (CPA):** **B / R2C01 (hybrid)** leads at $17.65, about 24% below the control (calculated). **Low confidence; not yet a proven win.**
- **What B actually did (higher confidence):** it kept the control's post-click conversion (3.95% vs 3.77%) **and** got a much stronger hook (37.7% vs 24.5%) and cheaper clicks. That's the pattern the Round 1 hypothesis predicted. It's still practice data.
